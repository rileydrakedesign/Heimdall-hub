OpenAI is shifting resources away from long-term research toward improving ChatGPT, prompting departures of senior staff.

Vice-president of research Jerry Tworek left after seven years, saying he wanted to explore types of research that are hard to do at OpenAI.

Model policy researcher Andrea Vallone joined Anthropic.

Economist Tom Cunningham left, suggesting OpenAI was straying from impartial research to focus on work that promoted the company.

Researchers who don't work on large language models are having compute requests denied or granted amounts insufficient to validate research.

Teams working on Sora and DALL-E feel neglected.

In December, Altman declared a code red after Google's Gemini 3 outperformed OpenAI on benchmarks and Anthropic's Claude made strides in coding.

My Take

This is what a $500 billion valuation does to a research lab.

OpenAI started the generative AI boom, but now they're scrambling to keep up with Google and Anthropic while burning $9 billion a year with no profitability until 2029.

Nvidia is backing away from their $100 billion investment because even they have concerns about the business model.

They added ads to ChatGPT after Altman called ads unsettling in the context of AI not long ago.

Sora usage dropped 32% in January.

When you raise that much money at that valuation, the research mission becomes secondary to survival.

You stop exploring and start defending.

The people who want to do blue-sky work leave for places where they can get compute for projects not tied to the next product release.

OpenAI went from research lab to product company to platform company in three years.

The moat is 800 million users and hoping they don't leave.

That's not innovation.
That's lock-in.
