Post 1

stop asking who is winning AI.

start with the timeframe you care about.
because the answer changes.

1/ today looks like distribution and product polish
2/ next year looks like budget, GPUs, and execution speed
3/ ten years looks like infrastructure compounding and research pipelines

the common failure is mixing horizons.
you chase short term attention, then spend like you are building a decade long lab.
you get neither.

Post 2

chinese open weights have a US revenue constraint.

the discussion points to one blocker.
US enterprises avoid chinese AI APIs for security reasons.

so the wedge is open weights.
• ship the weights
• let teams run locally
• earn adoption without asking procurement to trust an external endpoint

if you sell into enterprise, benchmark wins feel good.
procurement wins pay the bills.

Post 3

deepseek R1 in jan 2025 mattered because it accelerated a pattern.

> one lab ships
> others reuse the ideas
> the newest release takes the crown
> then the crown moves again

deepseek did not fall off.
the field caught up.

if your strategy depends on a durable model edge, build for parity.
assume copy and catch up.

Post 4

hype does not predict usage.
habit does.

drivers called out in the discussion:
- name recognition
- muscle memory
- friends recommending the default
- teams standardizing on one workflow

if you build an AI product, obsess over the boring parts:
- onboarding
- defaults
- reliability
- switching cost

attention spikes.
habits compound.

Post 5

people will split AI subscriptions by trust boundary.

one bucket holds personal context.
- memory features
- long running history

another bucket holds work context.
- clean slate
- tighter privacy posture
- less mixing across projects

if you build an AI tool, support that split.
workspaces matter.
boundaries matter.
users do not merge personal and work context to make your product easier to sell.
