Post 1

google gets a structural advantage most labs never touch.

it owns the stack.
chips, data centers, the serving layer.

so when margins on NVIDIA GPUs squeeze everyone else, google routes around it.

> build to fit your own stack
> avoid paying supplier margin
> scale without renegotiating your unit economics every quarter

if you build an AI product, copy the principle, not the company.
own the bottleneck you pay every day.

Post 2

the speed vs intelligence trade off is not a debate.
it is two different jobs.

fast mode
- quick lookups
- simple explanations
- low patience tasks

slow mode
- work queries
- reference checking
- higher stakes decisions

1/ give users a toggle
2/ default to fast
3/ make slow feel worth the wait

people do not want one model for every moment.

Post 3

model quality is not the only reason US models feel better.
serving architecture shows up in the experience.

what they describe in this section
• fewer GPUs per replica makes a model slower
• slower replicas increase timeouts and weird failure patterns
• users blame intelligence, then churn

if you serve a model, treat latency as product quality.
people experience speed first.

Post 4

your best model pick changes by task.

the workflow they describe is multi tool by default.

- one model for fast explanations
- one model for code and deep thinking
- one model for real time lookup

you do not need loyalty.
you need a reliable map.

document your routing rules.
write them down like a checklist.
then follow them until you learn a better one.

Post 5

users do not switch models gradually.
they switch when a threshold breaks.

the pattern
1/ you use a model until it fails on your job
2/ you lose trust
3/ you move your workflow to the next one

if you ship a model product, retention depends on two things.
- how often it breaks
- how fast you fix the specific break

generic improvements do not save you.
fix the break that caused the switch.
