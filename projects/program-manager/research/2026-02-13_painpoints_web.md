## Iteration 1 — Objections driven by quotas/credits (buyers now expect limits + visibility)

### Pain points / objections
**Agent for X**
- Objection: “Are you going to throttle me / silently cap me / surprise-charge me?”
  - Market conditioning: Later explicitly exposes **AI credits** and per-profile post limits at lower tiers.
  - Source: https://later.com/pricing/

**Class Chat AI**
- Objection: “Will this scale to my school/district without chaos?”
  - SchoolAI frames scale plans around governance and observability (rostering, permissions, dashboards).
  - Source: https://schoolai.com/pricing

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: **usage visibility + caps** are part of the UX (credits counters, dashboards).
  - Sources:
    - https://later.com/pricing/
    - https://schoolai.com/pricing

### Channel / marketing tactics
- Pricing pages now function as “limit disclosure + trust” pages (explicit caps/credits reduce surprise).
  - Source: https://later.com/pricing/

### Trust / privacy concerns
- When limits exist, users assume there’s an underlying platform/policy constraint—vendors who explain *why* limits exist earn trust.
  - Source (fair-use framing exists in-suite pricing): https://metricool.com/pricing/

## Iteration 4 — Legal/parent concerns: COPPA mechanics (what procurement will ask)

### Trust / privacy concerns
**Class Chat AI**
- COPPA compliance requirements (practical checklist): determine if you collect info from kids <13, post compliant privacy policy, direct notice to parents, verifiable parental consent, security procedures.
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business

### Pain points / objections
- Objection: “We can’t risk getting this wrong; what’s your parental consent flow and data deletion story?”
- Objection: “Do you collect persistent identifiers / voice / images?” (COPPA defines these as personal information).
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: clear **data inventory**, **retention/deletion**, and **parent rights** workflows.
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business

## Iteration 5 — Classroom adoption reality: teachers get warned not to input sensitive data

### Trust / privacy concerns
**Class Chat AI**
- Common Sense Education’s ChatGPT review includes explicit guidance: **never input personal/sensitive/confidential info into a generative AI model**; be aware info can become training data; get parental permission before asking students to use independently.
- Source: https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/chatgpt

### Pain points / objections
- Objection: “My admin told me not to put student info into AI.” This becomes a hard blocker unless the product offers a credible privacy posture + contracts.
- Source: https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/chatgpt

### Channel / marketing tactics
- Winning tactic: ship “teacher-safe default” onboarding copy + a 1-page safety/consent guide that matches the language teachers are already hearing.
- Source (teacher guidance baseline): https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/chatgpt

### Agent for X — analog trust lesson
- Trust posture is partly about **explicit ‘what not to do’**. For Agent for X: a public “Automation Safety Facts” / “Do/Don’t” page reduces fear-driven churn.
