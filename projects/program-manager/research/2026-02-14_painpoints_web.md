## Iteration 1 — Packaging itself creates objections: quotas, scheduling horizon, and “custom training” upsells

### Pain points / objections
**Agent for X**
- Objection: “Will this turn my writing generic?” → creators want voice control and predictable output quality.
- Objection: “Will I outgrow the plan immediately?” → quotas create anxiety unless surfaced clearly.
- Evidence: Postwise tiers explicitly meter **AI-generated posts/month** and “custom AI voices”; higher tiers add “custom AI training.”
- Source: https://postwise.ai/pricing

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: “Batch write → schedule weeks/months ahead” is a default workflow; scheduling horizon is a paid feature.
- Evidence: Postwise gates **3 months vs 12 months vs unlimited scheduling** by tier.
- Source: https://postwise.ai/pricing

### Channel / marketing tactics
- Pricing page doubles as a workflow pitch (“dominate your social media presence” + calendar-based batching).
- Source: https://postwise.ai/pricing

### Trust / privacy concerns
- “Custom AI training” implies data/voice ingestion; users will ask **what’s used for training, retention, and deletion**.
- Source (tier includes “Custom AI Training”): https://postwise.ai/pricing

## Iteration 2 — Doc-chat tools expose “limits UX” (questions/day, conversations/day) and model access → new buyer expectations

### Pain points / objections
**Class Chat AI**
- Objection: “Will it stop working when I’m cramming for an exam / during finals week?” (daily question/convo caps)
- Objection: “Is the ‘good model’ paywalled?” (model menu + credits required)
- Evidence: AskYourPDF shows **questions/day, conversations/day, docs/day** and multiple models; some require credits.
- Source: https://askyourpdf.com/pricing

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: “Knowledge base / chat with multiple documents” is standard.
- Evidence: AskYourPDF includes “Chat with multiple documents at once (Knowledge Base)” on paid tiers.
- Source: https://askyourpdf.com/pricing

### Channel / marketing tactics
- Chrome extension as a distribution wedge is now normal.
- Evidence: AskYourPDF includes “Chrome Extension Access” in Premium/Pro.
- Source: https://askyourpdf.com/pricing

### Trust / privacy concerns
- AskYourPDF explicitly answers “Is my data secure?” and claims GDPR compliance — but buyers still need specifics (retention, training use, region, sub-processors).
- Source (FAQ + GDPR claim): https://askyourpdf.com/pricing

## Iteration 3 — “Safe to use” messaging is now a conversion surface (especially for automation/outreach)

### Pain points / objections
**Agent for X**
- Objection: “Will this get me banned / throttled / flagged?”
- Objection: “Automation feels spammy; I don’t want my account to look like a bot.”

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: tools offer **engagement + outreach automations** (auto-comment, auto-reply, auto-DM) once you’re paying for the top tier.
- Evidence: Taplio Pro bundles auto-DM / bulk DMs and lead database; Standard adds auto-reply/comment at scale.
- Source: https://taplio.com/pricing

### Channel / marketing tactics
- “Free extension” is an acquisition funnel (in-product distribution).
- Evidence: Taplio describes an extension as a “free add-on” with basic features; higher tiers “supercharge.”
- Source: https://taplio.com/pricing

### Trust / privacy concerns
- “Is Taplio safe?” appears in the pricing FAQ and frames safety as “behaves like a real user” + “no hacks.”
- Source: https://taplio.com/pricing
