## Iteration 1 — Objection is baked into tiers: “AI writer” and automation features raise quality + spam concerns

### Pain points / objections
**Agent for X**
- Objection: “AI-generated tweets often need editing” (quality + voice mismatch).
- Objection: top tiers bundle automation (Auto-DM / Auto-retweet / Auto-plug), which creates a *spammy / risky* perception.
- Evidence: review explicitly lists AI tweets need editing; and describes automation features.
- Source: https://www.nichepursuits.com/tweet-hunter-review/

### Workflow expectations
- Expectation: “write a month in one sitting → queue → schedule weeks/months ahead” is the default workflow.
- Evidence: review describes adding tweets to a queue and scheduling ahead.
- Source: https://www.nichepursuits.com/tweet-hunter-review/

### Channel / marketing tactics
- Competitors market “education collateral” as activation/retention (growth guide / templates / resources).
- Evidence: Tweet Hunter review notes “Tweet Hunter University” and free resources/guides.
- Source: https://www.nichepursuits.com/tweet-hunter-review/

### Trust / privacy concerns
- Automation features increase ToS/ban anxiety even if not explicitly addressed; users want explicit “what we do / don’t do” and caps.
- Evidence: automation list includes Auto-DM / Auto-retweet / Auto-plug.
- Source: https://www.nichepursuits.com/tweet-hunter-review/

### Trust / privacy concerns
**Class Chat AI**
- Institutions are trained to demand contractual and product guarantees:

  - encryption + MFA
  - access logging
  - deletion/retention policies
