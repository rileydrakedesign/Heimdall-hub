# Research Brief — 2026-02-14

Priority order:
1) **Agent for X**
2) **Class Chat AI**

## What changed / what we learned today (high signal)

### 1) Creator tools price “AI writing + scheduling horizon” as the core ladder (simple tiers win)
- Postwise tiers are anchored to **AI-generated posts/month** and explicitly gate **scheduling horizon** (3 months → 12 months → unlimited).
  - Source: https://postwise.ai/pricing

### 2) Doc-chat tools are training users to expect multi-dimensional limits (not just “credits”)
- AskYourPDF exposes multiple meters: pages/doc, upload size, docs/day, questions/day, conversations/day, and reply length.
  - Source: https://askyourpdf.com/pricing

### 3) Automation safety is now a conversion question — and competitors answer it inside pricing
- Taplio includes an explicit “Is Taplio safe?” FAQ, framing safety as “behaves like a real user” / “no hacks or shortcuts.”
  - Source: https://taplio.com/pricing
- Third-party review language reinforces the same objection: Tweet Hunter review warns about “over-automation and spammy tactics.”
  - Source: https://softwareoasis.com/tweet-hunter-review-monetize-twitter/

### 4) Edu trust posture continues to trend toward governance + consent + vendor contracts
- FERPA + gen-AI guidance highlights consent (when needed), data minimization, third-party agreements, transparency, and audits.
  - Source: https://nmu.edu/ctl/understanding-ferpa-context-generative-ai-guide-faculty

## Implications for our two products

### Agent for X
- Packaging should be legible like Postwise (simple ladder), but differentiate on trust:
  - explicit meters (posts/replies/DMs), plus scheduling horizon as a tier knob
  - “Automation Safety Facts” page + in-product caps that prevent spammy behavior
  - show *why* guardrails exist (account protection, ToS alignment, cost predictability)

### Class Chat AI
- Product + pricing should reflect “limits UX” expectations:
  - plan comparison includes pages/doc, upload size, questions/day, conversations/day
  - model provenance (“which model answered”) becomes part of trust
- Institutional adoption requires a procurement posture:
  - data minimization defaults, consent guidance, vendor agreements, transparency + auditability
